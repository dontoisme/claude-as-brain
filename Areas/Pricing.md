# Pricing

**What this covers:** Contract terms, tier structure, discounting policy, and renewal pricing. Not deal-level negotiation — that's sales.

**Why I own it:** Pricing changes need product input on packaging, and nobody else is tracking the through-line across decisions.

---

## Current State

Enterprise is annual-only as of March 2025. Mid-market and below are flexible by policy, though sales appears to be defaulting to annual across all tiers regardless.

The annual-only decision is under quiet pressure and has not been formally revisited.

## Insights

### 2025-04-18

The March annual-only decision was explicitly conditional — Dana said "revisit if churn moves." Two things have happened since, and neither triggered a revisit because nobody defined what "moves" meant:

1. Mid-market renewal friction, direct from customers ([[Meetings/20250402 - Riverside Renewal]])
2. The cash position that justified the decision has changed since H1 planning

The rationale was never load-bearing on its own — it was load-bearing *given* a cash constraint that no longer applies the same way. That's worth naming plainly: the decision may still be right, but the argument for it isn't the argument we made.

Opened cab-ex2 to revisit properly. Also need churn by contract term (cab-ex4) — that's the evidence that would settle it either way, and it's striking that we've had this debate twice without it.

### 2025-04-02

Sales is applying annual-only beyond its intended scope. The March decision covered enterprise; mid-market was deliberately left flexible. In practice the distinction isn't reaching the field.

Possibly enablement rather than pricing. Haven't disentangled it. → [[Days/20250402]]

### 2025-03-12

Annual-only for enterprise. Cash flow predictability through H2. Marcus flagged mid-market price sensitivity; noted but not tested against data. → [[Meetings/20250312 - Pricing Review]]
